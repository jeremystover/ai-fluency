# AI 301 · The Specialist — HRBP track, exploration (pre-outline)

**Status:** working exploration, not an outline. First of the role tracks. The point of going
first is to find out what "role depth" actually means before any skeleton gets imposed on the
roles that follow.

**Method:** start from the job, not from the curriculum. Describe what an HRBP's year actually
contains, find where AI meets it, then subtract everything 101 and 201 already taught. What
survives the subtraction is the course.

---

## 1. What an HRBP actually does

Not the job description — the calendar. An HRBP aligned to a business unit spends their year on:

- **Advising and coaching managers.** The largest single block, and mostly conversational: a
  manager brings a problem, the HRBP helps them think. Output is often not a document at all.
- **Employee relations.** Complaints, investigations, performance problems, terminations. Low
  volume, extreme stakes, heavy documentation, occasional litigation.
- **The performance cycle.** Calibration prep, review quality, promotion cases, PIPs.
- **The comp cycle.** Merit planning, off-cycle requests, promotion economics, equity.
- **Org design and change.** Reorgs, spans and layers, role definition, and the communications
  cascade that follows.
- **Reading their population.** Engagement survey results for their unit, exit interviews,
  skip-levels, attrition patterns.
- **Sitting in the leadership team** as the people voice — often the only person in the room
  whose job is the second-order effect.

## 2. What's distinctive about it

Five properties that aren't true of People work generally, and that a role course has to be
built on:

**They advise; they don't own.** Influence without authority. A large share of an HRBP's real
product is *preparation for a conversation they're about to have* — not an artifact. No course
in this curriculum has yet treated AI as preparation for a conversation, and it may be the
single highest-frequency use in the role.

**Their inputs are unstructured, sensitive, and continuous.** Manager venting, 1:1 notes,
investigation interviews, Slack threads, skip-level notes. Almost all Tier 3, some Tier 4, and
it arrives daily rather than in batches.

**Their population is small.** An HRBP covers 80–400 people, often with sub-teams of six.
Every aggregate analysis brushes against the small-N problem that 101 M4 flags in a sentence
and this role hits weekly. "Themes from my unit's survey" is frequently *not* an aggregate.

**Their documentation has legal weight.** ER notes, PIP language, investigation summaries, and
termination rationale can end up in discovery. Provenance of a sentence can matter years later.

**They are already the verification layer for other people's AI.** The managers they support
are shipping AI-drafted reviews, PIPs, and JDs at them right now. This is a live problem with
no content anywhere in the curriculum, and it is unmistakably an HRBP problem.

## 3. The subtraction — what 101 and 201 already own

The failure mode for a role course is 201 with HR nouns. Every candidate topic below has to
state its *delta*: what it teaches that a learner who finished 201 doesn't already have.

Already owned, and not to be re-taught:

| Covered by | What it already gives an HRBP |
|---|---|
| 101 M4 | Data tiers, the shown-to-the-person test, redaction discipline |
| 101 M6 | The four failure types, verification sized to stakes, the high-risk surface |
| 101 M7 | Assist vs. decide, the traveling test, bias as fidelity |
| 101 M8 | Accountability, disclosure by reader's stake, the agreement layer |
| 201 M2 | Context packs — the durable briefing library |
| 201 M3 | Pipelines with checkpoints — *including survey verbatims → themes → exec summary* |
| 201 M4 | The verification budget, matched to failure mode |
| 201 M6 | People data in production; the boundary sheet |
| 201 M7 | The operating rhythm, measurement, retirement criteria |

Two consequences worth stating plainly. **Pipeline mechanics are done** — 301 must not re-teach
chaining, and the survey-verbatims pipeline is literally 201 M3's worked example. And **the
operating-rhythm module is done** — a "your HR calendar as workflows" module would be 201 M7
wearing a costume. Both temptations are strong; both are out.

## 4. Candidate topics, ranked by how much they earn their place

Ranked by the test: *would an HRBP read the module description and think "finally, someone
understands my actual job"?*

**A. The advisory prep engine — AI as sparring partner before a hard conversation.**
The manager coaching session, the leader who wants to fire someone this week, the conversation
you have to have with a VP about their favorite. Rehearsal, objection anticipation, framing
options, the question you haven't thought of. *Delta:* nothing in 101/201 treats AI as
preparation for a live human exchange — everything so far produces artifacts. This is the most
frequent HRBP use and the least covered. Highest confidence of the set.

**B. Coaching the managers who already use AI.**
The AI-drafted performance review that says nothing. The manager who pasted a complaint into a
consumer chatbot. The PIP with a fabricated policy citation. *Delta:* 101 taught the learner to
verify *their own* output; this is about being the person who catches, corrects, and teaches
*other people's* — with the specific scripts and the specific line-drawing an HRBP needs. Also
the natural bridge to 401, and a genuinely unserved topic anywhere.

**C. ER documentation that survives scrutiny.**
Investigation notes, summaries, termination rationale. Where AI helps (structure, consistency,
completeness checks — *"what did I fail to ask?"*), where it must stop (credibility
determinations, conclusions, characterizations of a person), and what discovery does to a
document whose provenance is uncertain. *Delta:* 101 M6 gave verification; this gives the
document types, the completeness use nobody thinks of, and the discoverability problem.
**Needs counsel review — more than 101 M7 did.**

**D. Small-N: reading a population you know personally.**
Your unit is 90 people; the "aggregate" analysis of the six-person design team is not an
aggregate. Where population reading stops being safe, how to tell a manager problem from a team
problem, and what a pattern across four resignations is and isn't evidence of. *Delta:* 101 M4
flags small populations in one line; for this role it's a weekly judgment, and the
interpretation question ("what is this evidence of?") is untouched by any module.

**E. The performance cycle.**
Calibration prep, auditing review *writing* for vague or coded language, promotion-case
consistency, PIP construction. *Delta:* the sharp and teachable distinction between AI reading
what *managers wrote* (assist — it's about the writing) and AI rating *people* (never). Strong
content; partially derivative of 101 M7's line, which is why it ranks below C.

**F. Org design and change.**
Scenario structuring, spans and layers, stakeholder impact mapping, the comms cascade and its
FAQ. *Delta:* one of the few genuinely low-risk, high-leverage AI surfaces in the role — it's
about structures, not individuals — right up until the moment it's about who gets cut, which is
the line the module exists to draw. Good module, less distinctive.

**G. What doesn't delegate.**
A closing module on the judgment that stays entirely human, and on being fast everywhere else to
buy time for it. *Delta:* mostly synthesis. Earns a slot as a course close, not on novelty.

### Deliberately not modules

- **The HR cycle as workflows** — 201 M7 already owns the operating rhythm.
- **Survey pipelines** — 201 M3's worked example. The *interpretation* survives inside D; the
  mechanics don't come back.
- **A comp module** — comp analytics is a People Analytics track topic; the HRBP's comp work is
  mostly cases and conversations, which A and E already cover.

## 5. What this suggests the course actually is

A shape is showing, and it isn't the one a template would have produced. The HRBP's 301 is
mostly about **conversations, judgment, and other people's AI use** — not about building more
workflows. Three of the top four candidates (A, B, D) have no artifact-producing pipeline in
them at all.

That matters for the two open design questions:

**Activities.** 101 and 201 grade artifacts. A and B don't naturally produce one. Options: grade
the *preparation* (the brief you built before the conversation, the coaching script, the
correction you'd send a manager), or grade a transcript of the learner using AI as a sparring
partner and reflecting on where it helped and where it flattered them. The second is more
honest to the skill and harder to fake than it looks. Worth deciding before drafting.

**Storage.** If role tracks are separate courses (`ai301-hrbp`), the path shows a learner only
their own — which needs a role→track mapping off the `roleLabel` we already collect at intake,
and a fallback for roles with no authored track. If they're one course with role-variant blocks,
the `variant` column exists but the path would show every role's modules to everyone. Leaning
separate courses; decide before seeding, not before drafting.

## 6. Open questions for review

- **Eight modules, or fewer?** The subtraction may have left six real ones. A tight six-module
  301 per role is cheaper to maintain and easier to complete than a padded eight, and nothing in
  the product requires courses to be the same length.
- **How much does the track assume 201?** An HRBP who tests out of 101 and skips 201 will still
  want their role track. Do modules carry "you'll want 201 M3 first" pointers, or does the track
  stand alone?
- **Does A (advisory prep) want the tutor rather than a read?** It's the module whose subject
  matter — practicing a conversation — the product's own chat surface performs better than prose
  can. Possibly the first module in the curriculum that should be *primarily* the tutor, with
  the read as support.
