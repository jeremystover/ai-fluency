# AI 301 · The Specialist — Talent Development / L&D track, exploration (pre-outline)

**Status:** working exploration, not an outline. Fourth role track, after HRBP, Recruiting/TA, and
Comp & Benefits.

**Method:** start from the job, not from the curriculum. Describe what an L&D professional's year
actually contains, find where AI meets it, then subtract everything 101, 201, and the three
existing 301 tracks already teach. What survives the subtraction is the course.

**Why this role was expected to be hard:** the authoring brief predicted that content production —
the most AI-saturated work in the entire People function — would invert the diagnosis module the
way the comp track's did. That prediction is correct, and it turned out to be an understatement.
This is the first role where the AI-exposed surface is not an adjacent risk to the craft. **It is
the craft.**

---

## 1. What a Talent Development professional actually does

Not the job description — the calendar. Across a year:

- **Taking requests.** "We need a training on X." From a manager, a function head, an exec, or a
  post-incident debrief. The volume is constant and the quality is not.
- **Needs analysis.** Deciding whether the request is a training problem at all — or a process
  problem, a staffing problem, an incentive problem, or a manager problem wearing a training
  request's clothes.
- **Instructional design.** Objectives, assessment, sequencing, practice design, spacing and
  retrieval, transfer. The actual discipline, and the part with a real research base.
- **Content production.** Slides, e-learning modules, scripts, job aids, facilitator guides,
  video, assessments, workbooks. **The largest single block of hours in the role.**
- **Facilitation and delivery.** Live and virtual sessions, train-the-trainer, cohort management.
- **Program management.** Leadership programs, onboarding, mentoring, high-potential cohorts.
- **The LMS.** Assignment, completion tracking, compliance reporting, audit support, the annual
  fight with the platform.
- **Compliance training.** Mandated, auditable, and legally specified in ways most other L&D
  content is not.
- **Skills architecture.** Taxonomies, capability frameworks, career paths, proficiency scales —
  the fastest-growing part of the role and the least examined.
- **Measurement.** The annual attempt to say whether any of it worked, which has never gone well.
- **Vendor management.** Content libraries, authoring tools, the LMS, coaching platforms.
- **And now: owning AI enablement for the entire company.** Every function's AI upskilling lands
  in this team's queue, and the subject matter changes faster than the team can build for it.

## 2. What's distinctive about it

Five properties that aren't true of People work generally, and that a role course has to be built
on.

**Their core craft output is precisely what generative AI does best.** This is the inversion. The
HRBP track's second module lands on a reassuring finding: the HRBP's core territory — employee
relations, org design, contested judgment — is the *least*-penetrated part of HR. Run the same map
for L&D and it points the other way. Learning and development is the third-highest AI use-case
concentration in the function at 17%, behind only recruiting and HR technology `[V]`, and 71% of
L&D professionals report experimenting with or integrating AI `[V]`. **The diagnosis module for
this role cannot say "you're not behind, calm down." It has to say something harder.**

**The field's canon has an unusually high density of confidently-held falsehoods — and the model
has read all of it.** Learning styles. The learning pyramid's retention percentages. 70-20-10.
MBTI in leadership development. These are not fringe: the *majority* of instructional designers
still endorse learning styles `[V]`, and the retention percentages were never in Edgar Dale's work
at all — someone superimposed them around 1970 and the attribution to NTL has no traceable source
study `[V]`. Every other role's AI risk is that the model doesn't know their organization. **This
role's AI risk is that the model knows their field, and their field is disproportionately wrong.**

**They have never been able to prove their work matters, and now they can produce ten times more of
it.** Level 1 reaction surveys dominate; behavior and business-results evaluation are rare and
expensive `[V]`. This is the volume trap — already taught in 201 and HRBP M3 — but with a
property no other function has: **there is no instrument that would notice.** An HRBP producing
more decks and no more decisions eventually gets caught by the decisions not happening. An L&D
function tripling its catalogue with no capability change has never had a measurement layer that
would surface it, and the completion rate will look excellent.

**Their subject matter now decays faster than their production cycle.** L&D owns AI enablement, and
AI changes quarterly. A course built on a twelve-week design cycle is stale at launch. This is the
first role in the curriculum where **content half-life is a design constraint rather than a
maintenance annoyance** — and it is the only role whose day job is structurally the same problem
this course solves for itself with the volatile layer.

**They are building a decision system about people and calling it a taxonomy.** Skills inference,
proficiency scoring, readiness signals, "who's ready for the next level." Nobody in L&D thinks of a
skills framework as a decision engine, which is exactly why 101 M7's assist/decide line has never
been applied to it. When a model infers an employee's skills from their work history and that
inference gates access to a development program or a stretch assignment, it is making a decision
about a person through a door nobody is watching.

## 3. The subtraction — what the rest of the ladder already owns

The failure mode for a role course is 201 with different nouns, and this role is the most exposed
to it in the whole curriculum, because its bulk work is document production and **201 M3 is
document pipelines.**

| Covered by | What it already gives an L&D professional |
|---|---|
| 101 M4 | Data tiers, redaction discipline, the shown-to-the-person test |
| 101 M5 | Prompting as briefing — role, task, context, format |
| 101 M6 | The four failure types, verification sized to stakes |
| 101 M7 | Assist vs. decide, bias as a property of training data |
| 101 M8 | Accountability, disclosure by reader's stake |
| 201 M2 | Context packs — **a "course style guide" pack is this, exactly** |
| 201 M3 | Pipelines with checkpoints — **SME transcript → structured module is this, exactly** |
| 201 M4 | The verification budget matched to failure mode |
| 201 M6 | People data in production, the boundary sheet |
| 201 M7 | The operating rhythm — **an "L&D calendar as workflows" module is this, exactly** |
| HRBP M3 | The volume trap, botsitting arithmetic, the vendor-claim teardown |
| HRBP M4 | Adversarial rehearsal — sycophancy as a *conversation* problem |
| HRBP M7 | Manager capability, and the AI-coaching RCT |
| Recruiter R4 | Designing signal that's expensive to fake |

Five consequences worth stating plainly, because each one kills a module that would otherwise look
obviously right for this role:

1. **"Build an e-learning module with AI" is 201 M3.** The single most obvious L&D topic is already
   taught. Content-production mechanics do not come back.
2. **"Your L&D annual calendar as workflows" is 201 M7.** Out.
3. **"A reusable pack for your course voice and standards" is 201 M2.** Out.
4. **The volume trap is taught.** L&D's version only earns a place because of the missing
   instrument, not because production got cheaper. If the module is "AI makes more content," it is
   a repeat. If it is "your function has never had a way to detect that the extra content is
   worthless," it is new.
5. **"Your assessments are now free to pass" is Recruiter R4 with different nouns.** Expensive-to-
   fake signal design is owned. The L&D-specific residue — that assessment was already the weakest
   link in the practice and has now become structural — survives as a lesson, not a module.

That is five strong candidate modules eliminated by subtraction, which is roughly what the brief
predicts should happen and is the clearest sign the discipline is working.

## 4. Candidate topics, ranked by how much they earn their place

Ranked by the test: *would an L&D professional read this module description and think "finally,
someone understands my actual job"?*

**A. Premise inheritance — the model agrees with your field's bad ideas because you briefed it
rather than asked it.** The strongest candidate in the set, and the verification pass is what made
it strong. The naive version of this module was "the model has absorbed your field's myths and will
repeat them." That version is wrong. Richter et al. (2025) found LLMs identify neuromyths **more
accurately than educators do** when asked directly — and then reproduce them anyway when the myth
arrives embedded in a practical request `[V]`. Their worked example is, verbatim, an instructional
designer's Tuesday: *"I want to improve the learning success of my visual learners. Do you have any
ideas for teaching material for this target group?"* — and every model tested produced visual-
learning material without flagging the premise.

*Delta:* this is a **fifth failure type** beyond 101 M6's four. Not a fabricated specific, not
plausible-but-wrong reasoning, not a confident gap, not a stale fact. The output is fluent,
internally consistent, well-sourced, and wrong at the foundation — because the foundation came from
the user. And it inverts 101 M5: briefing well is taught there as the core skill, and **the better
and more specific the brief, the more unexamined premises it smuggles in.** Expertise is the risk
factor. Nothing in the curriculum teaches this, and no other role has a canon dense enough with
false premises to make it a weekly problem.

**B. The request that shouldn't become a course.** The highest-leverage hour in L&D is the intake
conversation where someone decides whether this is a training problem at all — and cheap production
just removed the friction that used to kill bad requests. Building a course was expensive, and
**the expense was doing work nobody counted: it was the filter.** Now the honest answer ("this is a
process problem, training won't fix it") competes against an afternoon's work, and it will lose
unless the discipline is explicit.

*Delta:* nothing in 101 or 201 covers deciding whether to build at all. It rhymes with the recruiter
track's intake module — and that rhyme should be stated rather than hidden — but the mechanism is
different: R3 is about errors compounding downstream through a scorecard, this is about an economic
filter disappearing.

**C. Evaluation that can come back negative.** The measurement void, aimed at the one thing that
would fix it. The teachable move is not the four-question audit — that is HRBP M3 — it is designing
evidence that could actually fail, which the field has avoided for fifty years and can no longer
afford to.

*Delta:* HRBP M3 measures a workflow's honest time cost. This measures whether a *program* did
anything, which is a different question with a different literature and a fifty-year record of
being dodged.

**D. The floor — mandated content, accessibility, and what you licensed.** Required as a position
in the arc, and the content is entirely role-specific. State harassment-prevention statutes specify
duration, interactivity, and content elements `[V]`; WCAG 2.1 AA is the practical accessibility
benchmark and AI-generated media fails it in predictable ways (uncaptioned video, decorative alt
text, colour-only signalling); and the licensing questions — what your content library's terms
permit, who owns AI-generated course content, whether your authoring tool trains on your material —
have no analogue in the other three tracks.

**E. The skills taxonomy as an unrecognized decision system.** Strong and genuinely unserved, with
one complication: it sits on the boundary with the unwritten People Analytics track. Best answer
for now is a lesson inside D rather than its own module, since the teachable content is 101 M7's
line applied to a surface nobody recognizes as a decision — which is a lesson-sized idea, not a
module-sized one.

**F. Designing for a subject that changes quarterly.** Novel, and this role's live problem. Risk of
thinness, and risk of drifting into 201 M7's operating rhythm. Survives as a lesson in the closing
module, framed as *what you build when you know it will be wrong in ninety days* — with a
self-referential payoff, since this course marks its own volatile layer for exactly that reason.

**G. AI enablement for the whole company.** L&D's newest and loudest mandate — and mostly not a 301
topic. Cross-functional capability building, change management, and governance is how 401 · The
Translator is defined, and the manager-facing slice is already HRBP M7. The 301-level residue is
the craft question in F.

### Deliberately not modules

- **Content production with AI** — 201 M3.
- **The L&D calendar as workflows** — 201 M7.
- **A course-standards context pack** — 201 M2.
- **Detecting AI-assisted assessment answers** — Recruiter R4, and the detection reflex is
  addressed there.
- **AI coaching program design** — the RCT lives in HRBP M7. See the boundary question below,
  because there is a real argument it is in the wrong track.

## 5. What this suggests the course actually is

A shape emerged that no template would have produced, and it came from timing the job rather than
categorizing it.

L&D work runs a chain: **request → diagnosis → design → production → delivery → transfer →
evidence.** The hours have always concentrated in the middle — production is the biggest block in
the role. The value has always concentrated at the ends: deciding what is worth building, and
proving it did something.

**AI collapsed the middle.** Which means the two ends — the two things this field is historically
worst at, and the two it has always been able to skip because the middle absorbed the time — are
now the entire job.

That gives the track its through-line, its decomposition axis, and its two central depth modules in
one move. The barbell is not a metaphor imposed on the role; it is what the timesheet says once you
sort it.

It also settles a design question the other tracks had to argue about. The HRBP track opens with a
short diagnosis module and decomposes in M2; the comp track merges them into one. **This track
merges them, because here the diagnosis *is* the decomposition finding** — "the middle of your chain
collapsed" is simultaneously the news and the map.

## 6. Boundary questions this exploration raises

- **Talent Management — inside this track, or homeless?** The brief flags it as unresolved.
  Exploration finding: **mostly not here.** Performance cycles and calibration are HRBP territory
  and already covered there; succession and high-potential identification are decision-about-people
  surfaces that belong with the skills-inference lesson in the floor module. Talent Management is
  not a missing track so much as a set of lessons already distributed across two.
- **The AI-coaching RCT may be in the wrong track.** HRBP M7 uses de Haan, Terblanche & Nowack
  (2026) to argue that allocating human coaching is an equity decision. That is a good use. But the
  study is about *development program design*, and its central finding — narrowly-scoped bots work,
  broad ones don't, and outcomes track the coachee's starting self-efficacy — is L&D's core
  professional question, not the HRBP's. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by whoever
  authored first. This track's outline does **not** claim it.
- **Skills inference and the People Analytics track.** Whoever authors People Analytics will want
  model evaluation and inference quality. The line proposed here: People Analytics owns *whether the
  inference is any good*; this track owns *what happens to a person because of it*.
- **Does this track need a stronger prerequisite than the others?** It is the only track whose
  subtraction removes its own bulk work. A learner who skipped 201 M3 will feel a hole where
  content production should be, and the honest answer is "that module exists, go take it" rather
  than re-teaching it thinly here.
