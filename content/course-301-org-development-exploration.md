# AI 301 · Organisational Development / Org Design — exploration

**Status:** exploration, written to decide whether this is a track, a merge, or a set of placements.
Raised by the course owner (register A-2) with a specific thesis: *"a lot of the change in
organisations will mean re-orgs, so this could become more important."* Two merge options were
proposed alongside it — fold into Talent Development as *TD & OD*, or fold into HRBP, since OD often
falls to business partners.

**The test, from the brief:** is there a *job* here with a week and a year to describe? And since
`trackForRole` resolves each learner to exactly one 301 track, **overlap with a sibling is not an
argument against a track** — no learner sees two.

**What makes this exploration different from the Talent Management one, and harder.** TM was tested
against nine sibling 301 tracks. OD has to be tested against those *and* against **AI 401**, which
shipped ten modules of what is, in substance, organisational design taught to leaders. That is the
whole difficulty, and §1 is about it.

---

## 1. The 401 problem, stated first because it decides most of the answer

AI 401 · The Translator covers, in shipped content:

| 401 module | What it teaches |
|---|---|
| M1 · Load Paths | Structures as load-bearing or decorative; the ones nobody designed are the ones holding weight |
| M4 · Ownership Contracts | Role differentiation; ownership written at the workflow, not the reporting line |
| M5 · The Invisible Work Audit | **Span of control as a variable rather than a ratio**; coverage not headcount; orphaned work |
| M6 · Integration Capacity | The rate at which an organisation can absorb change, and governing speed against it |
| M7 · Codification | Practice that survives a resignation |
| M8 · Designed Need | Genuine interdependency as a design object |
| M10 · The Operating System | Metrics, decision rights and rituals as the instruments that make behaviour real |

**That is a great deal of org design**, and it is well done — M5's span-of-control lesson is better
than most OD training on the subject.

But there is a clean line through it, and it is the line the course names itself:

> **401 teaches you to keep a structure standing while it changes. It does not teach you to design a
> new one.**

Every 401 artifact is diagnostic or protective: *what is load-bearing, what is dissolving, what must
hold, what would tell me it stopped holding.* The capstone is called **"What We're Keeping
Standing."** The verb is *keeping*.

**Nothing in 401 designs a target state.** There is no operating model, no structure options
appraisal, no spans-and-layers exercise, no selection process, no consultation, no redeployment.
401's learner is explicitly *"responsible for how this lands in teams I don't run"* — a person
without the authority to redraw anything. An OD practitioner is the person who redraws it.

**That distinction is real, and it is exactly the distinction that made Comp a track.** Nobody argued
comp was covered because HRBPs have pay conversations; comp is a track because *designing the pay
architecture* is a job. Same structure here — with the added weight that 401 is not role-split, so
an OD learner would take both, and the two would have to be deliberately different rather than
accidentally overlapping.

## 2. What the job actually is

**The week:** running a structure options appraisal for a leader who has already decided and wants
support; a spans-and-layers analysis; facilitating a leadership team that cannot agree what a
function is *for*; writing a target operating model; designing the consultation and selection process
for a restructure; the survivor conversation nobody scheduled.

**The year:** one or two structural changes, an operating-model review, a culture or engagement
programme cycle, and — increasingly — being handed an AI-driven headcount thesis and asked to make it
organisationally coherent.

**Size threshold.** A distinct OD function appears reliably above roughly 5,000 people. Below that it
sits with the CHRO, a senior HRBP, or an external consultancy. **That threshold is higher than for
any track we have built**, and it is the strongest argument against, so it is stated here rather than
buried in §6.

## 4. The AI angle — one strong idea, one warning sign

**The warning sign first, because it is a pattern this curriculum keeps hitting.** The most obvious
AI observation about org design is that *a target operating model is now free to produce and no
cheaper to agree.* Ask a model for a People operating model for a 6,000-person company and you get a
credible one in ninety seconds. What you cannot get is why *these* boundaries, *here*.

**That is CP-7's agreement problem, one function over** — already written, already shipped in TD M4.
If it were the best idea available here, this exploration should end in placements, exactly as
Talent Management did. **It is not the best idea available.**

### The idea that is genuinely new

> **AI is decoupling the role from the work, and the org chart is the last artifact to notice.**

An org chart is a set of boxes. Every box assumes a *role* — a stable bundle of tasks, held by a
person, describable in a job description, comparable to other boxes for pay and level. That
assumption is what makes structure designable at all.

AI removes tasks from bundles **at different rates within the same role**, and it does so unevenly
across people holding the same title, because adoption is individual. So:

- **The box stops describing the work**, while continuing to determine pay, level and reporting line.
- **Comp's job architecture, TD's skills taxonomy and the org chart all rest on the same unit** — and
  they are drifting apart at different speeds, each maintained by a different function, none of whom
  owns the drift.
- **Two people in the same box now do materially different jobs**, which is a structural problem
  presenting as a performance problem.

That is uncovered, it is specific, it is consequential, and it is *organisational design's problem
rather than anyone else's* — comp will notice it as a levelling anomaly, TD as a skills-data quality
issue, the HRBP as a fairness complaint, and none of those framings reaches the cause.

### And the one with a sharp practical edge

> **You can now generate a re-org's rationale faster than you can build consent for it.**

The deck, the options appraisal, the FAQ, the manager talking points — all cheap. Consultation,
selection fairness, and the legal record are not, and have not moved at all. **The gap between how
fast a restructure can be argued and how fast it can be legitimately executed just widened**, and
that gap is where re-orgs go wrong. This is also where OD touches the one genuinely uncovered legal
surface in the subtraction, which matters because every other track's floor is already built.

## 5. The two merge options, assessed

### (a) Talent Development & OD

**The case for is better than it first looks.** Many organisations do combine them ("Talent & OD"),
and **TD M2 is already the OD hinge** — its whole subject is *"this is a process problem, training
won't fix it,"* and the loss of the economic filter that used to make that argument winnable. That is
the classic OD/L&D boundary, taught well.

**But the case against is decisive.** TD's spine is the learning portfolio and its economics — triage,
diagnosis, evaluation, the floor, the defence. Org design is a second spine with a different unit of
analysis, a different week, and a different learner. Bolting it on gives one 6-module track two
unrelated arguments and makes it the only track with two audiences. **A track with two spines has
none.**

**Verdict: no.** But one lesson is owed to TD M2 regardless — see §7.

### (b) HRBP

**Stronger on audience.** OD genuinely does fall to senior HRBPs in mid-sized organisations, and HRBP
already owns org change advisory.

**And still wrong, for the reason that decided Comp.** The HRBP does OD *reactively* — this leader,
this restructure, this quarter. The OD practitioner does it *architecturally* — the operating model,
the design standard, the process that governs every restructure. **That is precisely the
conversation-versus-architecture distinction that made Compensation a track rather than an HRBP
module**, and applying it inconsistently here would be the same error in reverse.

There is also a practical problem: HRBP is already the longest track at 7 modules **and** the fallback
destination for `other`, so it carries the most heterogeneous audience on the platform. It is the
worst place to add specialist depth.

**Verdict: no** — but two HRBP placements are owed. See §7.

## 6. Verdict

**A track — provisionally, and gated on a verification pass that has not been run.**

This is the opposite conclusion to Talent Management, and the reasons are specific rather than
enthusiastic:

1. **The subtraction leaves four substantial uncovered areas**, not three overlapping ones.
2. **There is a job with a week and a year**, and it is architectural rather than advisory.
3. **The sharpest AI idea available is genuinely uncovered** — role/work decoupling — rather than a
   restatement of something already shipped.
4. **The re-org's legal and process surface is the largest uncovered floor in the curriculum.**

**Three honest reservations, in order of weight.**

**The audience threshold is the highest of any track.** A distinct OD function needs ~5,000 people.
Every other 301 track serves the 1,000–10,000 band comfortably; this one serves its top half. **This
is a market question and the course owner is better placed to answer it than this document is** —
which is exactly how the Talent Management verdict said such questions should be asked.

**401 already serves much of this learner.** An OD practitioner takes a 301 *and* 401. The two must be
deliberately different — 401 keeps structures standing, 301-OD designs them — and if that line blurs
during drafting the track becomes a longer 401 for a smaller audience. **That line should be a stated
tripwire in the outline**, the way the comp M3 → People Ops M5 → Analytics M2 chain has one.

**Verification has not been run, and this field's literature is the most consultancy-dominated in
HR.** Operating-model and re-org content is overwhelmingly produced by firms selling the service.
The Talent Management exploration hit exactly this and it is what killed its anchor. **No outline
should be written until a verification pass establishes whether there is citable primary evidence
for the role/work decoupling thesis and for re-org outcomes** — and if there is not, the honest move
may be a track that argues from mechanism and says so, as 401 M2 did when its statistic failed.

**Recommended sequence:** verification pass → if it survives, full outline → build. Not straight to
outline.

## 7. What the 300 content needs regardless of the track decision

These are owed whether or not OD becomes a track, because they fix things that are wrong or thin
today. Filed as CP items.

**CP-9 · TD M2 — the diagnosis that is now free to ignore.** M2 already teaches that the economic
filter which made *"this is a process problem"* winnable is gone. The AI-specific second half is
missing: **the structural diagnosis is now also free to produce**, so an L&D lead can generate a
credible org-design critique in an afternoon — and generating it is not the same as having standing
to deliver it. The best hook in the curriculum for the OD/L&D boundary, and one paragraph closes it.

**CP-10 · HRBP — the restructure you are asked to support after it was decided.** HRBPs are handed
structural decisions and asked to make them work. The AI version has a specific shape: the rationale
was generated, it is coherent, and coherence is not evidence. What the HRBP can actually do is name
what the design assumes is true and ask whether anyone checked.

**CP-11 · HRBP or Comp — two people in the same box doing different jobs.** The presenting symptom of
role/work decoupling reaches the HRBP as a fairness complaint and comp as a levelling anomaly.
Neither currently has the frame that explains it. **This is the most valuable of the three**, because
it is a real thing already happening in organisations that nothing in the curriculum explains.

**And one thing that is not owed:** no change to People Ops, Analytics, LER or Defensible. Their
boundaries with OD are clean.

## 8. What this exploration is not

It has not run verification, it has not tested the module map, and it has not asked whether the
product's target customer has this function at all. **It answers "is there a job and is it
uncovered?" — yes to both — and stops there deliberately**, because the Talent Management exploration
showed the value of separating that question from the outline, and because A-1's error came from
answering a coverage question with a grep instead of a read. Every coverage claim in §3 was checked
by reading the cited module.
